Use the past event as a starting point, not an open invitation

The October 10, 2026 date discussed above has now passed. Treat the event description as a historical example of how to prepare for career exploration, rather than assuming registration is still open. You can still use the same preparation method for a future opportunity: choose a role, identify the skills it requires and collect a small piece of evidence showing what you can already do.

For a fictional example, a retail worker wants to investigate inventory work. Instead of writing “I love cannabis,” they describe an instance of organizing stock, correcting a discrepancy and communicating the result. That example demonstrates a transferable skill without claiming specialized training they do not have. The next question is which requirements the actual employer lists for the actual opening.

Create one useful sample of your work

Choose a small practice artifact relevant to your interest: a clear customer-information sheet, a sample inventory checklist or a short explanation of how you would document a task. Keep it fictional and label it as a sample. Accuracy and readable organization tell a prospective employer more than a crowded page of industry buzzwords.

Your decision prompt: can you connect a stated job requirement to evidence from your experience? If yes, prepare the example. If no, identify a learning step before claiming the skill.
