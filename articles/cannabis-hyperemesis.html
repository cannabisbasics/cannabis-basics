Cannabinoid Hyperemesis: When Cannabis and Repeated Vomiting May Be Connected

Understand the pattern, share the full history and seek care without trying to diagnose yourself from a symptom list.

Cannabis Basics · Published October 12, 2026 · 5–7 min + learning checks

A paradox worth understanding

Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome, or CHS, involves recurring severe nausea, vomiting and abdominal pain associated with cannabis use. It can feel confusing because people may associate cannabis with nausea relief. NIH describes hot showers or baths as something some people use for temporary symptom relief; that does not resolve the underlying problem. Stopping cannabis is central to recovery when CHS is the diagnosis.

Repeated vomiting has many possible causes. A clinician needs to assess the whole situation rather than accept a self-diagnosis based on an internet checklist. Not everyone who uses cannabis develops CHS, and not every person with vomiting and cannabis exposure has CHS. A history of cannabis use is relevant information, not a reason to dismiss other potential explanations.

Give clinicians the timeline

Write when episodes began, how long they last, whether you can keep fluids down and what happens between episodes. Include the products, routes, frequency of use and changes over time. Tell the clinician about medicines, alcohol and other substances as well. If you have been treated before, share what the evaluation found and what treatments seemed to help or did not help.

It may feel uncomfortable to describe cannabis use during an urgent visit. A clear statement can be brief: I use cannabis, here is how often, and these are the products I can identify. This helps the care team ask better questions. If you cannot recall details while ill, a label photo or a family member's help can be useful, with your permission.

Temporary relief is not a treatment plan

A person notices that a hot shower helps briefly, then interprets that relief as a reason to manage each episode at home. The missing question is why the vomiting keeps returning. Temporary improvement does not establish safety, rule out dehydration or identify the cause. Avoid prolonged or very hot bathing as a substitute for assessment. Do not add more cannabis to test whether it will settle the stomach.

Seek urgent medical care for persistent severe vomiting, inability to keep fluids down or signs of dehydration. Severe abdominal pain, blood in vomit, fainting or other emergency symptoms need prompt evaluation. In a life-threatening situation, call 911. This article is a guide to the conversation and pattern, not an at-home treatment protocol or a way to avoid emergency care.

Plan the follow-up conversation

If a clinician diagnoses or suspects CHS, ask what stopping cannabis involves, how to manage symptoms safely, when to return for care and what follow-up is needed. People using cannabis for another medical concern should also discuss alternatives for that concern. A plan should address both the vomiting and the reason cannabis was being used, so the person is not left to improvise.

Use your journal to document episodes and clinical instructions rather than testing new products during illness. Read the tolerance-and-withdrawal guide if stopping is difficult, and bring your record to the appointment. The general medical-cannabis video provides background; it is not a CHS diagnostic tool. The most useful action is a complete history and a clear plan with a qualified healthcare professional.
